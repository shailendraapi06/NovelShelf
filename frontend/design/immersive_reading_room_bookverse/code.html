Carlos Ruiz Zafón 42

I still remember the day my father took me to the Cemetery of Forgotten Books for the very first time. It was the early summer of 1945, and we walked through the misty streets of a Barcelona trapped beneath skies of pewter and cold ash, breathing in the scent of damp masonry and yesterday’s rain.

"Daniel," my father whispered as we halted before an ancient carved portal on Calle del Arco del Teatro, "what you are going to see today must remain between us. Not even your friend Tomás may know of this place."

A man with the gaunt features of an ascetic welcomed us in silence. We descended a grand spiral staircase that coiled into the subterranean belly of the city. Down in that labyrinth of vaulted arches and stone colonnades lay a sprawling necropolis of literature: millions of abandoned volumes, towers of leather-bound folios spiraling toward the high gloom.

"Every volume, every book you see here has a soul," my father explained, touching the spine of an aged folio embossed in gilt. "The soul of the person who wrote it and of those who read it and lived and dreamed with it."

"According to tradition," continued my father, resting a firm, warm hand upon my shoulder, "the first time someone visits this sanctuary, he must choose one book—any book he pleases—and adopt it, ensuring that it will never perish, that it will always remain alive."

For almost half an hour I roamed the shadowy galleries, breathing the venerable aroma of old wood and forgotten paper. I brushed my fingers against brittle vellum, cloth bindings stamped with forgotten coats of arms, and gold-leaf emblems flaking like dead skin.

And then, among hundreds of forgotten titles, it found me. It was tucked away in a corner of an arched alcove, bound in wine-colored morocco leather, its gold lettering dimmed by decades of cellar dust: La Sombra del Viento, by Julián Carax.

I pulled it carefully from the shelf. A faint shudder coursed through my fingertips, as though the volume were alive, its slumber stirred by the pulse of an unsuspecting ten-year-old child.
